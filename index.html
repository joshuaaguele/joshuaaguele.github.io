04 / UNDERGRADUATE RESEARCH

Tuberculosis surveillance.
Recognising meaningful change.

My BSc Statistics thesis at Ambrose Alli University compared CUSUM and EWMA using tuberculosis data.

CUSUM · cumulative sum

Accumulates deviations from a reference value to help detect persistent changes in a monitored process.

EWMA · exponentially weighted moving average

Combines current and previous observations with weights that decrease for older observations. Sensitivity depends on the chosen smoothing and control limits.

This work established my interest in statistical monitoring. The illustration explains the methods; it does not establish which performed better in the study.

07 / LABOUR MARKETS & DIGITAL TRACE DATA

What can search behaviour
tell us about work?

Google Search Trends as a proxy for the Dutch labour market.

I compared search interest in “vacatures” with official CBS vacancy statistics for the Netherlands across 16 quarters, from 2022 to 2025.

The analysis uses quarterly aggregation, percentage changes, Pearson correlation, OLS regression, diagnostic checks and sensitivity analysis in Excel and Python.

08 / VALIDATING THE PROXY

A signal needs
scrutiny.

The central question is how closely a digital trace reflects the phenomenon we want to understand.

01

Measurement error

Does searching for “vacatures” represent active jobseeking, general curiosity or something else? Search interest and the number of vacancies measure different things.

02

Coverage error

Who is represented in online searches, and who is missing? Internet use and platform behaviour limit how broadly the findings can be generalised.

With only 16 quarterly observations, findings are exploratory. Correlation does not establish causality or reliable forecasting performance.

14 / ABOUT ME

Statistics.
With a human context.

My interest in health data began with statistical questions—and grew through hospital work.

I hold a BSc in Statistics from Ambrose Alli University, Nigeria. My undergraduate thesis used tuberculosis data to compare CUSUM and EWMA methods for early detection. That work introduced me to the challenge of recognising meaningful changes in health data.

At Berta Medical Centre, I worked with patient records, analysed hospital data and prepared reporting for the hospital’s National Health Insurance Scheme (NHIS) department. I also recommended medical software, migrated records and trained doctors and nurses. During an earlier internship, I coded a BMI calculator in MATLAB that doctors used.

These experiences shape my growing focus on biostatistics and health data science: combining statistical reasoning, programming and an understanding of how clinical data are recorded. I am interested in developing this further through survival analysis, longitudinal data and carefully evaluated prediction models.

My interests also extend beyond healthcare. My independent work includes environmental and spatial analysis across Dutch municipalities, Google Trends and CBS labour-market research, and a SQL and Power BI dashboard. The reports, preprints and repositories let you explore that work directly.
